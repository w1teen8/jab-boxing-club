02Round

Why the jab comes first

A jab is the first punch anyone is taught, and it is the most technical one in the sport. A straight line out, the same line back, nothing wasted. Learn it properly and the rest of boxing has somewhere to attach.

Three weeks on one punch

Your first month is footwork, guard and the jab. Not because it is the easy part. Because every other punch is built on top of it, and skipping it is how people end up swinging.

Nobody spars in month one

No contact, no rankings, no fitness test at the door. You will not be matched against anyone, and no coach will ask what you used to do at school.

Everyone is new in the beginner hour

The first-time group is all first-timers. The whole room is learning the same punch at the same speed, which is the only reason it stops being uncomfortable.

Your first class, minute by minute

Six rounds, three minutes of work and one of rest, the way the hour actually runs. Skip ahead if you do not want to wait out real time.

Ready 3:00 1 of 6

Warm-up

Rope, shoulders, hips. Eight minutes in you will be warmer than you expected and mildly annoyed about it.

The clock runs against real time, so it stays honest if you switch tabs.

Read the whole class as text
  1. Warm-up, 3 minutes. Rope, shoulders, hips. Eight minutes in you will be warmer than you expected and mildly annoyed about it.
  2. Stance and guard, 3 minutes. Feet first, then hands. You will feel like you are standing wrong. Everyone does for about a week.
  3. The jab, 3 minutes. One punch, both hands, a hundred times. Your shoulder will mention it tomorrow.
  4. Pads with a coach, 3 minutes. First real contact. The coach calls a shot, you throw it. Nobody is hitting you back.
  5. Bag rounds, 3 minutes. Three minutes alone with the bag. Your breathing will fall apart, and that is normal.
  6. Stretching, 3 minutes. Floor, quiet, the lights go cool. The part people skip and then regret.

One minute of rest sits between every round. Drink, breathe through your nose, stay on your feet.
