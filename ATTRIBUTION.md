# Footage

Every moving and still image on this page comes from one source:
[Mixkit](https://mixkit.co), under the
[Mixkit Free Stock Video License](https://mixkit.co/license/#videoFree), which
allows free use in commercial and non-commercial projects without attribution
or permission. Recorded here anyway.

Nothing here was shot at JAB, because JAB is not a real club. The page says so
in two places.

## One source, one grade

Each clip was trimmed, cropped, silenced and put through the same curve, so
twelve separate shots read as one roll of film rather than a stock grid:

```
# work
eq=saturation=0.20:contrast=1.10:brightness=-0.015:gamma=0.97,
colorbalance=rs=0.035:gs=0.005:bs=-0.035

# rest (night, stretch)
eq=saturation=0.18:contrast=1.10:brightness=-0.02:gamma=0.97,
colorbalance=rs=-0.03:gs=0.00:bs=0.055
```

Red survives that desaturation only where it matters, which is why the wraps
and the jab still carry it.

## Every still is a poster frame

`assets/stills/` holds no photographs. Each `.webp` is the first frame of its
own loop in `assets/video/`, exported through the identical crop and grade. So
the still a visitor sees before a loop attaches, on mobile, or under
`prefers-reduced-motion`, is the same image the video opens on, and there is
nothing to cross between the two states.

## About the people on screen

These are performers in licensed stock footage. They are not coaches, they do
not work at any gym called JAB, and the names printed under them are invented.
The licence permits the use; the page carries the disclosure twice, in the band
under the hero and in the footer, naming the coaches as invented and the people
as stock performers, so no visitor can reasonably read a strip as a real
person's real job.

Keep that disclosure if you reuse this. If you swap these for photographs of
actual people, get their consent first, and note that the Mixkit and Unsplash
licences do not themselves grant rights to a recognisable person's likeness for
every purpose.

The credit also appears on the page itself, under the row, which is what the
BY and BY-SA terms ask for.

**None of these people is presented as connected to JAB.** Each is named as
what they actually are, a champion, and the band opens by saying in plain words
that they have nothing to do with the club. That line is not decorative. It is
the difference between citing a public figure and inventing an endorsement, and
it stays if you reuse this.

What is deliberately **not** here: these people as coaches, as members, as
anything implying they train at or endorse this gym, and any fight footage of
them. Broadcast footage of modern professional boxing is owned by the
promoters and broadcasters, no free licence to it exists, and there is no
version of putting it behind this page that is both legal and honest.
